Chapter 05 — The Baby They Planned to Steal

Chapter 5 of 10

The search of North Valley continued through the night.

The more rooms police opened, the less the place resembled a treatment center and the more it looked like a machine built to make inconvenient people disappear behind professional language.

Investigators recovered forged medical records, altered embryo-storage forms, sedatives, fake consent documents, and video files showing Eleanor being moved between private facilities under different names.

They also found a nursery.

It was locked behind a door with no window.

Inside were newborn clothes, unopened bottles, diapers, a bassinet, and a hospital bracelet already printed with Vanessa Whitmore's name as the mother.

On a shelf sat a folder labeled DELIVERY PLAN.

Eleanor was scheduled for a medically unnecessary early induction in four weeks.

The baby would be moved to a private maternity suite.

Vanessa would disappear from public view for forty-eight hours, then reappear with Alexander's biological child.

There would be photographs.

A birth announcement.

A private DNA test confirming Alexander's paternity.

And no one would question the mother because the mother was the part of the story they had spent eight months rehearsing.

One document was clipped to the back of the plan.

It authorized the destruction of Eleanor's active medical file after delivery.

No transfer facility was listed for her.

No recovery plan.

No future patient number.

She was never supposed to leave North Valley alive.

Alexander read the page once and gave it back to Torres.

He did not speak for several minutes.

Then an officer came from Dr. Reed's office with another folder.

This one carried Alexander's name.

Inside was a medication schedule.

The compounds were not strong enough to knock him unconscious. They were designed to work slowly - confusion, memory gaps, fatigue, irregular heart rhythm, moments of disorientation that could be described publicly as stress.

Vanessa had recently begun giving him a nightly "sleep supplement."

If the plan succeeded, Alexander would deteriorate shortly after the baby was born.

A physician would certify him medically unable to lead.

Julian would become acting chairman.

Vanessa would control the child's trust.

Cynthia would control the fallback foundation.

Together, they would have enough voting power to dominate Whitmore Holdings.

"My wife has been drugging me," Alexander said.

Detective Torres did not correct the emotion in his voice, only the legal reality.

"The woman you married has been drugging you."

That distinction hurt him.

It also mattered.

Near dawn, a nurse asked to speak privately.

Her name was Marissa Cole. She had worked at North Valley for four months. She admitted she suspected Eleanor was being held illegally, but Dr. Reed controlled schedules, licenses, and recommendations. Staff who questioned him were dismissed.

Two weeks earlier, Marissa copied a video from the clinic server.

"I didn't know who to give it to," she said. "I was afraid the police would call him first. Everyone calls the doctor first."

Torres watched the file on a secured laptop.

The video showed Julian standing beside Eleanor's bed.

He held a photograph of Alexander and Vanessa at the gala pregnancy announcement.

"You will give birth," Julian told Eleanor. "Vanessa will take the child, and Alexander will finally become useful."

Eleanor's voice was weak but steady.

"You hate your brother."

Julian smiled.

"No. I hate standing behind him."

The video continued.

Eleanor told him Alexander would discover the missing foundation money.

Julian said he would not.

"Once the baby arrives, everyone will believe grief and exhaustion caused his breakdown. When he can no longer lead, I will become the brother who saves the family."

That recording connected Julian directly to the conspiracy.

But it opened another question.

What foundation money?

Torres asked Eleanor later that morning at the public hospital.

Eleanor was weak, but the sedatives were beginning to leave her system.

"Before the crash," she said, "I found payments leaving the Whitmore Family Foundation. Research clinics. Maternal-health programs. Charities that did not exist."

"Who authorized them?"

"Julian."

"Who received them?"

"Companies Cynthia created. Reed signed the medical invoices."

Alexander stood near the window.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

Eleanor looked at him for a long time.

"I tried."

The answer did not absolve him.

He knew that.

"Julian had already told you I was becoming paranoid. Reed diagnosed anxiety. You asked me to take the medication."

Alexander closed his eyes.

"I helped them drug you."

"You didn't know."

"I should have listened."

"Yes."

She did not soften the truth to protect him from it.

On the night of the mountain crash, Eleanor had left with copies of the financial records. Julian followed her. Her vehicle struck the guardrail but did not immediately burn. A private ambulance arrived before public emergency crews.

Dr. Reed was inside.

They took her to North Valley instead of a hospital.

Later, a burned body from an unrelated death was placed in the wreckage and identified as Eleanor through falsified dental records.

Alexander stared at her.

"Whose body did we bury?"

"I don't know."

There would now be another victim to identify.

Another family who had been lied to.

Before Eleanor could say more, Torres received a call from the clinic search team.

They had found evidence that Dr. Reed had done something similar before.

Not to Eleanor.

To someone else.
