Chapter 03 — The Name on the Ultrasound

Chapter 3 of 10

Alexander stared at the ultrasound as if the letters might rearrange themselves if he waited long enough.

They did not.

ELEANOR HALE.

The scan was dated five weeks earlier.

Three years earlier, Eleanor's car had gone off a mountain road during a storm. The vehicle burned before rescue teams reached it. Dr. Malcolm Reed had identified her through dental records. There had been no public viewing. The Whitmore family buried a sealed casket.

Alexander had spent nearly two years grieving before he met Vanessa.

Now he held a recent ultrasound carrying his dead wife's name.

"You told me she was dead," he said.

Dr. Reed's face had gone gray.

"She is."

"Then explain this."

"I can't."

Benjamin examined the clinic logo printed beneath the scan.

"North Valley Women's Care."

Dr. Reed looked at him too quickly.

Detective Torres saw it.

So did I.

The clinic was owned through a medical company connected to Reed. Attached to the ultrasound images was a photocopy of a missing-person report.

Six months earlier, a woman using the name Eleanor Hale had appeared at a women's shelter in northern California. She claimed someone had held her against her will. Two days later, she disappeared.

The photograph was blurry.

Alexander recognized her immediately.

"That's Eleanor."

Vanessa reached toward him.

He stepped away.

"Did you know?"

She said nothing.

"Vanessa. Did you know Eleanor was alive?"

Her silence answered before her mouth did.

Cynthia moved between them.

"My sister needs a lawyer."

"Your sister needs to answer me."

"Not here."

"You built this here."

That was the first moment Alexander raised his voice.

The ballroom went silent again.

Detective Torres separated Vanessa, Cynthia, Reed, and Julian for preliminary questioning. The guests were asked to remain while statements were collected. My team locked down the dressing corridor and preserved every access log from the previous twelve hours.

Lily stayed close to me.

"Is the gold lady in trouble?"

"Yes."

"Because of the pillow?"

I looked across the room at Alexander, still holding the ultrasound.

"Because the pillow was part of something much bigger."

Julian remained unusually calm.

He crossed the ballroom toward his brother.

"Come home with me," he said. "Let the police handle this."

Alexander looked up.

"My first wife may be alive."

"I understand."

"Do you?"

A small change passed across Julian's face.

I might have missed it if I had not spent years watching people lie to security cameras.

Lily did not miss it either.

She leaned toward me.

"Mom, that man was in the hallway too."

"Which man?"

She pointed directly at Julian.

"He gave the doctor a key."

Julian heard her.

His calm disappeared.

I went to the security console and pulled the electronic access history for the Whitmore dressing suite.

At 7:42 p.m., Julian's executive guest credential opened the door.

At 7:45, Dr. Reed's temporary credential entered the same room.

Vanessa and Cynthia arrived later.

Detective Torres placed the log in front of Julian.

"Why were you in the dressing suite before Vanessa?"

"I was checking it for my sister-in-law."

"And Dr. Reed?"

"He needed a private room."

"For a patient who had not arrived yet?"

Julian did not answer.

One of my officers came from the service corridor holding an evidence bag.

"Claire, we found this behind the dressing-room vanity."

Inside was a medical transport bracelet.

The clinic name matched the ultrasound.

NORTH VALLEY CARE CENTER.

On the reverse side, written in black marker, was a room number.

214.

Dr. Reed closed his eyes.

Detective Torres stepped toward him.

"Where is Room 214?"

He remained silent.

Alexander picked up the evidence bag.

"If Eleanor is alive and you know where she is, I will spend every dollar I have making sure you never practice medicine again."

Julian immediately cut in.

"Threatening him won't help."

Alexander turned toward his brother.

"You seem very interested in keeping him quiet."

Julian's phone rang.

He rejected the call.

It rang again.

Torres glanced at the screen before he could hide it.

NORTH VALLEY CARE CENTER.

Julian tried to put the phone away.

The detective stopped him.

A voicemail notification appeared almost immediately. During the handoff, the speaker activated.

A frightened woman's voice filled the ballroom.

"Mr. Whitmore, the patient in Room 214 is awake. She heard the gala broadcast. She knows Vanessa was exposed."

No one moved.

The caller drew a shaky breath.

"She keeps asking for Alexander."

Julian's face lost all color.

Then the woman added one final sentence.

"And she says you're the man who tried to kill her."
