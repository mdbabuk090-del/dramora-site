A Street Child Held Out My Sister's Hair Clip. My Sister Died Nine Years Ago.

A dirty little girl reached for the jeweled clip in Evelyn Hart's hair, then opened her own hand and showed a second one — scratched, bent, and identical. Only two were ever made. The other belonged to Evelyn's sister, whose car was pulled from a ravine nine years ago with her blood inside and no body.

Chapter 01 — The Girl at Bellamy Café

Chapter 1 of 12

Evelyn Hart noticed the girl because she did not belong in Bellamy Café.

Not because of her age. Children came through the terrace all the time, dragged behind parents with shopping bags and expensive sunglasses. This girl was different. Her gray dress was clean in places and ground-in dirty in others, as though someone had tried to wash it quickly in a sink. There was dried mud at the hem. A faint bruise circled one wrist. Her dark hair had been combed with fingers, not a brush.

She stood beside Evelyn's table and stared.

Evelyn set down her coffee. "Can I help you?"

The girl said nothing.

Her eyes moved to the jeweled clip above Evelyn's right ear: a slender silver leaf, its edges lined with small clear stones, a larger pear-cut stone set at the center where the veins met.

Then the child reached toward it.

Evelyn caught her wrist on instinct.

"Hey. Don't touch me."

The girl flinched so hard that Evelyn released her at once.

"I'm sorry," Evelyn said, softer. "But you can't just—"

The girl opened her other hand.

A second silver leaf lay in her dirty palm.

Evelyn stopped speaking.

The clip was scratched and dulled with age, one leaf tip slightly bent. But she knew the shape. She knew the uneven rim around the center stone. She knew the tiny flaw at the base of the clasp because she had teased her sister about it when they were seventeen.

There had only ever been two.

Their mother had commissioned them from an old jeweler in Prague after a summer the sisters spent there together. One for Evelyn. One for Mara.

Mara had been dead for nine years.

The girl looked at Evelyn's hair and then at the clip in her palm.

"I have a hair clip just like that."

Evelyn's fingertips went automatically to her own.

Her throat tightened.

"Where did you get that?"

"My mom."

A waiter passed behind them carrying a tray. Somewhere on the street a bus exhaled at the curb. Evelyn heard all of it and none of it.

"What's your mother's name?"

The girl hesitated.

"She said not to tell everyone."

"I'm not everyone."

The child studied her with an unsettling seriousness.

Then she said, "My mom said I'd find you here."

Evelyn felt the world narrow to the size of the table between them.

"Your mom?"

The girl nodded.

"She said you'd recognize the clip."

Evelyn's chair scraped sharply as she leaned forward.

"That's impossible." Her voice came out lower than she intended. "Where is she?"

The girl's fingers closed around the clip.

"I don't know."

Those four words frightened Evelyn more than the clip itself.

She glanced toward the pavement beyond the café railing. "Were you supposed to meet her here?"

"No. She brought me near here. She told me to walk the rest by myself."

"Why?"

The girl looked over her shoulder.

Evelyn followed her gaze.

Across the street, a black sedan was parked beneath a plane tree. Dark windows. Engine running.

"She said if the car followed her, it wouldn't follow me."

Evelyn's pulse kicked.

"What car?"

The child pointed before Evelyn could stop her.

The black sedan pulled away from the curb.

Evelyn stood.

"What's your name?"

"Lily."

"Lily what?"

The child swallowed.

"Lily Reed."

The surname meant nothing.

Then Lily reached into the pocket of her dress and pulled out a folded square of paper.

"Mom said I could give you this after you asked about the clip."

Evelyn unfolded it.

The message consisted of one sentence written in blue ink.

EVIE, IF LILY FOUND YOU, I RAN OUT OF TIME.

No one had called Evelyn Evie since Mara disappeared.

Her knees nearly gave way.

Below the sentence was a second line.

DO NOT TAKE HER HOME.

Evelyn looked up.

Lily was staring at the street again.

"Is that your mother's handwriting?" Evelyn whispered.

"Yes."

"When did she write this?"

"This morning."

Evelyn pressed the note between both hands to stop them shaking.

Nine years ago, police had pulled Mara Hart's car from a flooded ravine. They had found her coat, her blood, and a burned passport in the wreckage. They had never found a body.

For three years Evelyn had insisted that mattered.

For six more, everyone she loved had taught her to stop saying it.

She looked at Lily's face again.

The child had Mara's eyes.

Not similar eyes.

Mara's.

Evelyn grabbed her handbag.

"You're coming with me."

Lily took a step back. "Mom said you'd say that."

"Good. Then your mother still knows me."

"She said not to trust the man who comes when you call."

Evelyn froze.

"What man?"

Lily looked at Evelyn's phone lying beside the coffee cup.

"Your husband."

The phone began to ring.

ADRIAN flashed across the screen.

Synopsis

Evelyn Hart is having coffee at Bellamy Café when a child in a filthy gray dress stops at her table and reaches for her hair. Evelyn catches the girl's wrist and tells her not to touch. Then the child opens her other hand. Inside it is the twin of the clip Evelyn is wearing — one of two commissioned by their mother from a jeweler in Prague, one for Evelyn and one for Mara. Mara Hart has been dead for nine years. The girl carries a note in her mother's handwriting: EVIE, IF LILY FOUND YOU, I RAN OUT OF TIME. Below it, a second line: DO NOT TAKE HER HOME. What Evelyn pulls apart over the next twelve chapters is not a missing-person case but twenty-five years of the Hart Family Foundation moving vulnerable women through guardianship proceedings, a locked ward called Room 17, a grave with the wrong name on it, and a filing that says Evelyn Hart is not who she has spent her whole life believing she is. Part One of a continuing serial.
