Chapter 06 — Room 17

Chapter 6 of 12

For several seconds, no one moved.

Evelyn looked at Adrian.

Naomi looked at Adrian.

Adrian looked at Naomi.

Lily clutched the silent phone.

"She said it wasn't you," Evelyn said to Adrian.

"That's not the part that worries me."

Naomi's expression hardened. "She said whoever signed her in is close to Evelyn."

"It could still be Gideon through a proxy," Adrian said.

"Or someone else in the family."

Evelyn's mind moved through names.

Board members. Lawyers. Doctors. Family employees.

Then one face surfaced and she pushed it away immediately.

Dr. Miriam Bell.

Their family physician for twenty-five years. The woman who had sat with Evelyn after Mara disappeared and prescribed sleeping pills when she stopped functioning. The woman who had testified that Evelyn was emotionally competent to assume temporary control of the company.

The woman who had introduced their father to Dr. Voss.

"We need St. Agnes records," Evelyn said.

Adrian shook his head. "The official archive was destroyed in the fire."

"Official records," Naomi repeated. "Not all records."

She called a retired state investigator named Rosa Mendez, who had once handled medical licensing cases. By morning, Rosa found a name: Celia Monroe, former night nurse at St. Agnes.

Celia lived in Queens above a florist.

She refused to let them inside until Evelyn showed her the photograph of Mara outside St. Agnes.

The old woman's face changed.

"Nora," she whispered.

"Her name was Mara Hart," Evelyn said.

Celia closed her eyes.

"I know that now."

They sat in a tiny living room crowded with plants.

Celia's hands shook as she poured tea.

"Room 17 wasn't on the normal ward," she said. "It was in the west building. Private patients. No names on the doors. No visitors."

"Who authorized admission?" Naomi asked.

"Dr. Voss. Always Voss. But he needed family consent for long-term holds."

"Who signed Mara's?"

Celia looked at Evelyn with pity.

"The file said Evelyn Hart."

Evelyn swallowed.

"I was deceived."

"I believe you."

"Why?"

"Because Nora—Mara—believed you too. At first."

Evelyn's chest tightened.

"At first?"

Celia glanced toward Lily, who was sitting beside Adrian with a coloring book.

"When her memory returned, Voss showed her documents with your signature. He told her you had petitioned to keep her confined. He told her you had inherited everything and didn't want her back."

Evelyn shut her eyes.

Gideon had not merely hidden Mara.

He had made Mara believe Evelyn wanted her hidden.

"How long was she there?"

"Four years and seven months."

Evelyn opened her eyes.

"And Lily?"

Celia's mouth tightened.

"Born there."

The room seemed to drop away.

Lily looked up at the sound of her own name.

Celia lowered her voice.

"They tried to separate them. There was a transfer order for the baby when she was six weeks old. Mara became violent for the first time. Broke a nurse's nose. Nearly got herself moved to a state forensic unit."

"Where were they sending Lily?"

"A private adoption agency."

Naomi stopped writing.

"Which one?"

"Bright Harbor Family Services."

Adrian swore.

Evelyn turned to him.

"You know it?"

"Hart Foundation funded it."

"Gideon again?"

"No."

Adrian looked at Evelyn.

"Your mother started that grant program before she died."

Evelyn stared.

Their mother, Helena Hart, had died in a boating accident when Evelyn was twenty-one.

Three years before Mara's disappearance.

Celia rose and crossed to a desk.

From a locked drawer she removed a yellowed envelope.

"Mara gave me this the night she escaped. She said if anyone ever came asking with the other silver leaf, I should hand it over."

Evelyn took the envelope.

Inside was a single photocopied page from a patient ledger.

Room 17 — Nora Reed.

Guardian authorization: Evelyn Hart.

Supervising physician: Isaac Voss.

Emergency sponsor: H. Hart.

Evelyn's breath stopped.

"H. Hart?"

Naomi leaned closer.

"Could be Helena."

"My mother was dead."

Celia's face lost color.

"That's what I thought too," she said.

"What do you mean?"

The old nurse looked toward the window as though afraid someone might be listening from the street.

"Three months after Mara arrived, a woman came to Room 17 after midnight. She signed the private visitor ledger only once."

Celia's eyes returned to Evelyn.

"Mara called her Mom."
